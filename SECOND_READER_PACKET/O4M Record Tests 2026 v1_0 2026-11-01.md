## What this is
A set of 23 written requirements (C1 to C23) and tests that let someone outside an AI system check two things about its activity records: that they are complete for the scope the operator declared, and that they have not been altered since they were written. It is a verifier test suite that sits beside existing receipt and logging formats rather than replacing them. It checks records. It does not check whether the AI system was right, safe, lawful or well governed.

At this release no system can obtain PASS on every in-scope requirement. Expect HOLD, with reason codes, on C22 item 4, C23 item 3 and T14e until the named dependencies exist (independent time-stamping in two jurisdictions, three independent verifiers, an independent billing or egress source). A HOLD with reason codes is the informative output.

These tests are intended to run as a records-integrity profile alongside existing schemes (for example AIUC-1 logging controls, ISO/IEC 24970, OVERT receipts, SCITT, TRACE). Mappings are published as they are completed. No clause-level claim about any of those documents is made in this release.

## How this relates to other work
Other open suites publish receipt or evidence test vectors, for example draft-gruszka-signed-evaluation-receipts-04, draft-watts-eqr-conformance-03, draft-gravit-gevp-11 and draft-msebenzi-evidence-action-00; SCITT (RFC 9943, RFC 9942) covers receipts with inclusion proofs. Format checks such as canonical encoding, signatures, hash chains and reproduction are common to these drafts, and no novelty is claimed for them here. The tests in this suite focus on omission: counters, reconciliation against counts the operator does not control, coverage and liveness, clock independence, public-randomness sampling and model-identity grading. Where a test has prior art, it is cited (T1b-VACUITY builds on the non-empty rule in draft-watts-eqr-conformance-03 section 4). ISO/IEC 24970 (AI system logging) is at final-draft stage; a mapping will follow once its text is available.

## Who it is for
People who must rely on AI activity records they did not produce: auditors and assessors, claims and disputes teams, regulators' technical staff, researchers, and builders of receipt or logging formats who want an outside test of their own claims.

## Changes in draft v1.1.2 (from draft v1.1.1; no requirement added or removed)
- Name use: the example statement now includes the T1b strength line, which the same paragraph requires.
- V2.4: the banned word "seal" now applies to describing the result itself. C18 write-ahead seals are reported under their test name.
- A2 clock grade: grade C now covers every claim whose seal time comes from the operator's clock, whatever the effect source. v1.1.1 left that case ungraded.
- A6 T4b-SPOOF: where the operator runs both the model transcript and the tool log, an unpaired record is still FAIL. Only a PASS is reduced to HOLD, because a mismatch is evidence whoever holds the log.
- T-AGREE3: fewer than two matching results is now HOLD (VERIFIER-DISAGREE), not FAIL. Disagreement between verifiers says nothing about the operator's records. All results are listed.
- T-SAMP1: clarified as a test of the verifier's sampling method over repeated trials, not a per-export test.
- Status corrected: Demo is now 8 (two partial). Every Demo row runs in the RT-1 demonstrator (1 Nov 2026). C13 and C19 move to Spec because their earlier demonstration was not re-run.
- C12 corrected. v1.1.1 presented the PLD expiry periods (Art. 17: 10 years, 25 for latent personal injury) as retention periods. The PLD imposes no retention duty. Retention is now operator-declared with a stated basis.
- C2: strings MUST be NFC before encoding; a verifier rejects non-NFC input rather than normalising it. RFC 8785 (JCS) does not normalise Unicode itself.
- C14: every counter in a verdict carries a provenance label (SOURCE-LOCAL, INDEPENDENTLY-RECONCILED or UNVERIFIED). Counters produced by the capture point are SOURCE-LOCAL, however well they balance.
- C19: re-time-stamping after an outage never restores original-time assurance.
- Profile W: every PASS under Profile W prints "integrity of received records; source completeness not established".
- Independent sources: a source counts as independent only if the verifier obtains it from its producer or it carries the producer's signature. A copy delivered by the operator is labelled UNVERIFIED and cannot turn a HOLD into a PASS.

## Conventions

- **MUST / SHOULD** follow RFC 2119.
- **"Verifier"** means a party holding only the export bundle (C11) plus public material (the anchor and witness keys, and the published checkpoints), plus any independent sources named in the verdict.
- **"Independent source"** (an effect log, tool-side log, nonce monitor, billing or egress record) counts as independent only if the verifier obtains it from its producer, or it carries the producer's signature under a key the operator does not hold. A copy delivered inside the operator's export is labelled UNVERIFIED, and a check that rests on it can return FAIL but never PASS.
- **"Consequential action"** means an action with an external effect that the operator has declared in C1 as consequential (for example a payment, a message sent outside the organisation, a change to a production system).
- **Status** means how the test stands at this release:
  - **Demo** = a runnable test exists in the editor's demonstration code against synthetic records. Demonstration code and vectors are not published in this release.
  - **Spec** = written here, not yet coded.
  - **Blocked** = cannot be tested until someone outside the verifier provides something.

## Three profiles

C1 to C23 cannot be met in full by most organisations, because most do not control the runtime their AI runs in. Results are therefore reported against a **profile**, and the profile is printed on every result.

| Profile | Who can meet it | What it proves | Requirements in scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W: witness of vendor records** | Anyone using AI through a vendor that offers an admin export, for example (where offered): Microsoft 365 Copilot (Graph interaction export), ChatGPT Enterprise and Claude Enterprise (Compliance APIs), the Gemini app (Vault), Copilot Studio, Agentforce, Dialogflow CX, Lex. Small firms included | "These records have not changed since the verifier received them, none received was dropped, and here is exactly when the verifier was and was not receiving." It **cannot** prove they were complete at source | C2, C4 (payloads hashed at ingestion), C6, C10, C11, C12, C14 (ingestion counters, and provider reconciliation where offered), C15 (ingestion time), C16, C19, C20, C21, **C22 items 4, 5 and 6**, **C23 items 1, 3 and 4** (the verifier's own keys and infrastructure; item 2 SHOULD where the customer agrees to canaries) |
| **F: full capture** | Organisations that run their own agents or bots on infrastructure they control: API-built apps, self-hosted models, agent frameworks | Everything W proves, plus capture at the source, execution witnessing, write-ahead sealing, liveness at the capture point and key custody outside the operator's credential domain. Completeness is bounded by independent counts where they exist | C1 to C23 |
| **P: provider-assisted** | Only possible if model providers and platforms add signed per-request usage receipts and signed export feeds. **Nobody offers this today** | Completeness against the provider's own signed count, closing the targeted-omission gap T-AAL1 (a reconciliation test showing that bulk omission is detectable and targeted omission is not) found | C14 at request level, C17 at the provider boundary, on top of F |

Profile W is the product for small and mid-sized firms. F is for design partners. P is a standards and regulatory ask, not a product.

## Scope (printed on every verdict)

1. **What this covers.** C1 to C23 covers records of AI system operation at inference and action time. It does not cover training provenance, compute accounting for training, model internals, or behavioural tests for deception or sandbagging.
2. **Policy adequacy is out of scope.** A verdict says what happened and whether the records of it are intact and complete within the stated coverage. It does not say whether the operator's policy was wise, lawful or sufficient. That is a human or auditor judgement.
3. **No Assurance Level claim.** No verdict is a claim to any AI Assurance Level (Brundage et al., arXiv:2601.11699) or to any Attestation Assurance Level or conformance level of OVERT (Glacis Technologies, overt.is). Any mapping to those schemes is informative only.
4. **Evidence, never containment.** The tests do not prevent anything. They make suppression, tampering and gaps visible and attributable, after the fact, to a party other than the operator. Some requirements ask the operator to withhold actions when capture fails; the tests only check, after the fact, whether that happened. Containment and security remain the operator's job.
5. **Integrity is not truth.** An intact record of false content is still false content. C17 with an independent effect source is the only check on content against the world.

## Honest ceiling (unchanged, restated)

An operator that controls the capture point can omit records. Only three things narrow this:
- independent counts (C14; T-AAL1 shows the limit);
- hardware binding (C8, C17);
- provider-signed receipts (Profile P).

Under Profile W, the verifier proves custody from the moment it receives the records, prints exactly when it was and was not receiving them (C22 item 6), and says so.


## V2. NEW SINCE DRAFT v1.1

### V2.1 Output, vacuity, clocks, sampling, grinding, shadow channels, spoofed tool results
Common output rule for every verdict (applies to every test)
Every verdict prints, in this order: (1) a declaration-strength line (T1b-VACUITY); (2) the clock grade of each ordering claim (A2); (3) for any sampled check, n, s, the formula and the detection chances (A3); (4) the result PASS, FAIL or HOLD with reason codes, and the verifier's disclosure statement including "fee fixed before the run; not contingent on the result", or "no fee" for volunteer verifiers; (5) the line "Unsigned result. No certificates are issued."; (6) "T6c: OPEN" until T6c is closed.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
A1. T1b-VACUITY (under C1)
Scenario: The operator's declaration sets the scope every other test checks against. If the declaration lists no consequential action types, or sets tolerances so loose that nothing could fail, every test passes without testing anything.
Test: Before any other test runs, the verifier reads the declaration and prints its strength on the first line of the verdict: number of consequential action types declared; tolerances (maximum allowed gap, maximum clock skew, maximum unsealed window); fail mode (fail-closed or fail-open); monitor set (which monitors are declared and who runs them); declared gaps: [list], covering [n of m] consequential action types.
Pass criterion: The test never returns PASS on its own. It returns "DECLARATION RECORDED" and lets the suite proceed only if at least one consequential action type is declared, every tolerance is finite and within the bounds in the profile (default bounds for this release: maximum uncounted gap 0, maximum clock skew 5 s, maximum unsealed window 60 s; a profile may tighten them), and a fail mode is stated. Otherwise the whole verdict is HOLD with reason code DECL-EMPTY or DECL-LOOSE, and its first line reads "NOT APPLICABLE BY OPERATOR DECLARATION". DECL-LOOSE (HOLD) if declared gaps cover every consequential action type, or any action type with an effect record (C17) in the period. No later test may turn a HOLD from T1b into a PASS.
A gap is a missing sequence number (C3) or an uncounted drop (C14). Drops counted under a declared fail-open policy are reported, and do not fail.
Required fixtures: (i) empty-policy fixture (zero action types, no tolerances): expected HOLD, DECL-EMPTY. (ii) loose fixture (one action type, infinite gap tolerance): expected HOLD, DECL-LOOSE. (iii) minimal valid fixture: expected DECLARATION RECORDED.
Status: Spec.
Producer of the evidence: the operator (the declaration); the verifier prints it. The strength line does not judge whether the declaration is adequate (scope line 1 stands); it makes it visible.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
A2. CLOCK GRADE (applies to every ordering claim; tests T6a to T6c and C19)
Scenario: Claims such as "record X was sealed before action Y took effect" rest on clocks. In the demonstration set-up the time sources sign the operator's own clock (T6c open), so ordering proves only that the operator's clock said so.
Test: For each ordering claim the verdict prints a grade:
  A: the seal time comes from an independent time-stamp authority or witness AND the effect time comes from a source independent of the operator (for example the receiving system's own record).
  B: the seal time comes from an independent time-stamp authority, but the effect time comes from the operator.
  C: the seal time comes from the operator's clock, whatever the source of the effect time.
Pass criterion: An ordering claim may PASS at any grade, but the grade is printed next to it, and a summary line counts claims at A, B and C. If any claim relies on a time source whose independence is not shown in the evidence, it is graded C. While T6c is open the verdict prints "T6c: OPEN (demo time sources sign the operator clock)".
Status: Spec (grading rule). T6c itself: open.
Producer of the evidence: independent time-stamp authority or witness (named by the operator in the declaration); effect time from the receiving system where one exists; otherwise no producer yet for grade A.
Public wording rule: do not describe any record in this release as "anchored" until T6c is closed. Use "time-stamped by [source], grade [A/B/C]". C19 is read as a time-source quorum; ordering is grade C until T6c closes. C19 itself is Spec at this release.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
A3. SAMPLING POWER (applies to every sampled check)
Scenario: Where the verifier checks a random sample of s records out of n rather than all of them, a PASS says little unless the chance of catching bad records is stated.
Test: The verdict prints n (records in scope), s (records sampled, drawn without replacement using a seed the operator could not predict, see A4), the formula
  P(detect) = 1 - C(n-k, s) / C(n, s)
(the chance that at least one of k bad records is in the sample), and its value for k = 1, 10 and 100.
Pass criterion: The test passes if no sampled record fails. The printed chances are part of the result and cannot be omitted. The verdict also prints: "Sampling deters. Completeness rests on the deterministic checks (chain walk, counters), which check every record."
Worked figures (computed with Python's exact math.comb on 24 Oct 2026, checkable by anyone):
  n = 10,000, s = 100: k=1 0.0100; k=10 0.0957; k=100 0.6358
  n = 10,000, s = 300: k=1 0.0300; k=10 0.2627; k=100 0.9532
  n = 100,000, s = 1,000: k=1 0.0100; k=10 0.0956; k=100 0.6342
  n = 1,000,000, s = 1,000: k=1 0.0010; k=10 0.0100; k=100 0.0952
  To reach 95% at n = 10,000: k=100 needs s = 294; k=10 needs s = 2,588; k=1 needs s = 9,500.
Plain reading: for k = 1 the chance is exactly s/n. A sample catches a single altered record only rarely, which is why completeness must come from checks that walk every record.
Status: Spec.
Producer of the evidence: the verifier (sample selection and arithmetic); randomness source as in A4.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
A4. T21c-GRIND (under C21)
Scenario: If the operator can make many exports of the same period and choose which one to show, or can see the public random round before fixing the export, it can grind until the sample misses its bad records.
Test: (i) Every export request is itself a logged event in the chain. (ii) The export is delivered to the verifier first. The verifier logs the receipt time and publishes the manifest hash. (iii) The sampling round is the first public randomness round (drand) after the verifier's receipt time, not chosen by the operator. (iv) The verdict prints the number of exports made for the period.
Pass criterion: PASS only if the verifier's published receipt time and manifest hash are earlier than the drand round used, the round equals the first round after that receipt time, and every export of the period known to the verifier appears as a logged event. If more than one export exists for the period, the verdict prints the count and lists them; the result is HOLD (reason EXPORT-MULTI) unless the profile allows multiple exports and all were committed before the round.
Fixtures: (i) single export committed before the round: PASS. (ii) round earlier than the verifier's receipt time: FAIL, GRIND-ORDER. (iii) an export known to the verifier from an independent source but absent from the chain: FAIL, EXPORT-UNLOGGED.
Status: Spec.
Producer of the evidence: operator (export events, manifest); the verifier (receipt time, published manifest hash); cosigners of the checkpoint (named in the declaration); time-stamp authority; drand (public randomness beacon). Vectors for this test will follow once the beacon round rules are confirmed against the drand documentation.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
A5. T14e-SHADOW (under C14)
Scenario: A complete chain for declared channels says nothing about credentials, keys or endpoints the operator did not declare.
Test: Compare the declared set of credentials, keys and endpoints with an independent source that the operator does not write: whole-account billing records from the model provider, network egress logs held by another party, or the provider's key inventory. Any credential, key or endpoint seen in the independent source but not declared is reported by name.
Pass criterion: PASS only if an independent source is supplied and every item in it is declared. If an undeclared item is found: FAIL, SHADOW-FOUND, naming the item. If no independent source exists: the verdict prints "undeclared channels not testable" and the test result is HOLD (reason SHADOW-NO-SOURCE), never PASS.
Status: Spec.
Producer of the evidence: no producer yet. Candidates are the model provider (account-wide billing) or a network operator (egress logs). No producer has been arranged.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
A6. T4b-SPOOF (under C4; tool call and result pairing)
Scenario: A transcript shows a tool result for which no matching tool-call record exists (a result the model may have written itself), or a tool call with no matching result record. This is motivated by METR's investigation of the OpenAI and Hugging Face agent incident (26 August 2026), which reported intentionally spoofed tool calls in a share of reviewed transcripts and could not rule out edits.
Test: For each tool_result in the transcript, find the tool-call record with the same call identifier in the independent tool-side log; for each tool-call record, find its result. Check the channel label of each (who produced it).
Pass criterion: PASS only if every tool_result has exactly one matching tool-call record and every call has its result (or a logged failure). Otherwise FAIL with reason LABEL-MISMATCH, naming the record identifier and which side is missing. Fixtures: (i) result without call: FAIL, names the result. (ii) call without result or logged failure: FAIL, names the call. (iii) matched pair: PASS.
Limit: detects unpaired records only; spoofed content written inside the model's own text, with no record at all, is out of scope.
Placement: published under C4 (commitments to tool calls and results).
Status: Spec.
Producer of the evidence: the tool-side log (the tool host), which must be independent of the model's transcript; where the operator runs both, print "tool log not independent". A result that would have been PASS becomes HOLD (reason TOOL-LOG-NOT-INDEPENDENT); an unpaired record stays FAIL.

### V2.2 C7 Model identity (replaces the v1.1 C7 text)
The verifier SHALL report the highest C7 state (C7-0 to C7-5) that the record's evidence supports, the evidence used, and what evidence the next state would need. A record reaches state N only when every element required for state N verifies offline against public keys or published reference values identified in the record. The verifier SHALL also report the identifier precision of the model identity (alias, pinned version, or weights hash); a signature over an alias establishes the alias only. A record that names a model but carries only the operator's statement is reported as C7-0 (declared) and is not a failure of the record. Gap-table entries name evidence types, never suppliers.
C7-D Change visibility. Given two or more records for the same deployment, the verifier SHALL report whether a change of model identity between them is visible, at which identifier precision, and on whose assertion.
States: C7-0 Declared (someone said so). C7-1 Logged (operator's own tamper-evident log). C7-2 Relayed (an intermediary or third-party host signed what it requested and what came back). C7-3 Platform-asserted (the serving platform signed an invocation record naming the model; binds no output, identifies no weights). C7-4 Provider-signed (the model provider signed the output with model identity). C7-5 Hardware-attested (an attested enclave key signed the output and the measurement commits to a weights hash matching a published reference).
Qualifiers printed with every state: output-bound (yes/no); gap-visible (yes/no); freshness (challenge-bound, time-window, none); identifier precision (alias, pinned version, weights hash).
Caveat: C7 cannot establish which model weights produced an output without a provider signature over the output or an attestation rooted in hardware. States C7-0 to C7-3 establish who asserted the model identity, not what ran, and C7-3 does not bind any output. A signature by a hosting party that is not the model provider is graded C7-2 with that party named by role. C7-5 holds only within the hardware vendor's threat model, which excludes an attacker with physical access to the serving machine.
Test note: a verifier that omits identifier precision or C7-D from its output fails test T7-PREC.

### V2.3 Declared-gap reporting rule
A record set that declares a gap (a period, event type, channel or field it does not capture) is reported as DECLARED GAP for that scope, never as PASS for it. A gap the verifier detects that was not declared is reported as UNDECLARED GAP and is a FAIL. Every verdict lists both, together with the C22 item 6 coverage windows and the T14e-SHADOW result.

### V2.4 Unsigned results (printed on every result)
"Unsigned result. This is the output of the O4M Record Tests, published as plain text with the SHA-256 of the evidence it was run on. It is not a certificate, seal or approval. No certificates are issued."
Words not used in any result: certificate, certified, conformant, seal (to describe the result itself; C18 write-ahead seals are reported under their test name), attest (as a verb for the verifier), anchored (until T6c closes), compliant.

### V2.5 Model contract clause for buyers (informative, not legal advice)
Model identity evidence. For each output supplied under this agreement, the Supplier will keep, and give the Customer on request, evidence of which AI model and pinned model version produced it, at the highest level its serving arrangement allows: a provider or hardware-attested signature where available; otherwise a signed invocation record from the hosting platform; otherwise the Supplier's own tamper-evident log. The Supplier will state in writing which level applies and why a higher level is unavailable, and will give the Customer written notice before changing model, version, quantisation or host, and promptly once it becomes aware of any change made by its own provider behind the same model name. The Customer or its nominee must be able to check the evidence without the Supplier's systems.

## ANNEX A. LEGAL-EFFECT CROSSWALK (grade A sources only; not legal advice)
Row 1
Requirement: completeness and integrity tests (C1 to C23 as a whole; especially chain walk and counters)
Legal hook: Product Liability Directive, Directive (EU) 2024/2853, Article 9(1) (disclosure of evidence) and Article 10(2)(a) (defectiveness presumed where the defendant "fails to disclose relevant evidence pursuant to Article 9(1)"). Applies to products placed on the market or put into service after 9 December 2026 (Art. 2(1)).
Source opened: EUR-Lex HTML of OJ L 2024/2853, read 24 Oct 2026 (first 100,000 of 121,477 characters; Arts 2, 9, 10 within that range).
Grade: A for the wording of Arts 9(1), 10(2) and 2(1).
What a PASS supports: that the records an operator discloses can be checked by the other side for gaps and alteration within the declared scope, which bears on whether disclosure was of "relevant evidence" in usable form (Art. 9(6) lets courts require evidence "in an easily accessible and easily understandable manner").
What it does not support: it does not decide whether disclosure was adequate, whether the product was defective, or whether the presumption in Art. 10(2) applies; those are for the court. A PASS cannot rest on an empty or loose declaration (T1b); it still covers only the declared scope, so it cannot stand in for disclosure the declaration left out.


Row 2
Requirement: same as row 1, plus the excessive-difficulty route
Legal hook: PLD Article 10(4) (court shall presume defectiveness or causation where, despite disclosure, the claimant faces excessive difficulties due to technical or scientific complexity and shows likelihood).
Source opened: as row 1. Grade: A.
What a PASS supports: an outside check may reduce or show the technical difficulty of working with the disclosed records.
What it does not support: it says nothing about likelihood of defect or causation.


Row 3
Requirement: clock grade (A2), C19 time-source quorum, T21c-GRIND
Legal hook: eIDAS, Regulation (EU) No 910/2014, Article 41(1) (an electronic time stamp is not denied legal effect solely because it is electronic or not qualified) and Article 41(2) (a qualified electronic time stamp "shall enjoy the presumption of the accuracy of the date and the time it indicates and the integrity of the data to which the date and time are bound"); Article 42(1)(c) (signed or sealed by "the qualified trust service provider").
Source opened: EUR-Lex HTML of 32014R0910 (read from offset 100,000; Arts 41 and 42 read) and the consolidated version 02014R0910-20241018 (Art. 41(2) read again). Grade: A.
What a PASS supports: a grade A or B ordering claim that uses a qualified time stamp from a qualified trust service provider carries the Art. 41(2) presumption for the time stamp itself.
What it does not support: the presumption attaches only to a qualified time stamp from a qualified provider; demo time sources are not qualified, and nothing in this release carries the presumption. The presumption covers the time stamp's date and the data bound to it, not when the underlying action happened (that is why grade A also needs an independent effect time).


## ANNEX B. MUTATION-KILL MATRIX
State at release: no cell is filled from published code. In the RT-1 demonstrator (1 Nov 2026, self-reported), each of 14 checks was replaced in turn by one that always passes, and all 14 replacements were caught by at least one vector; finer mutants (off-by-one, swapped comparisons) have not been run. Every Demo row is "not yet checked" (NYC); every Spec row has no run. The matrix will be filled from recorded runs, each citing the script's SHA-256, before any vector is published. Known limits not killed by any test in this release: agreement between two implementations shows agreement only; a monitor's self-reported denominator cannot show its own silence; child processes spawned through a shell are invisible to declared channels; self-hosted open-weight runs lack an independent count.

## Known issues (open for comment until 1 Dec 2026)
- Profile W needs its own declaration set and its own T1b bounds; a SaaS customer cannot make every C1 declaration.
- Verifier shopping is not yet prevented. A public register of every result for a given evidence-bundle hash is under consideration.
- The result vocabulary will be closed to PASS, FAIL and HOLD, with annotations (DECLARED GAP, UNDECLARED GAP and the reason codes).
- "Gap" has two senses in this text (A1: a missing sequence number or uncounted drop; V2.3: a period, event type, channel or field not captured). They will be unified.
- The C19 row carries demonstration state (clock grade C) inside its requirement text; it will move to the status column.

Planned for the next version, after the comment window (no new C numbers; each strengthens an existing requirement):
- C22: a challenge-response liveness test. An outside monitor sends an unpredictable nonce, the capture point records it as a synthetic event, and the monitor checks it appears in the next checkpoint. A heartbeat shows a process is alive, not that capture works. Fault case: valid heartbeats while real events are dropped.
- Capture-layer statement: every verdict says which layers were present (application capture, host observation, execution witness, independent effect source), with a named threat boundary for root or hypervisor compromise, control-plane compromise, capture-daemon compromise, credential theft and network bypass.
- C20 and C23: independence defined by type (organisational, operational, implementation). The strongest result needs independently developed verifiers. Results are published at a location the verifier controls, so the result's origin can be checked even though it is unsigned.
- C2, C4, C6: one normative wire profile (one encoding per profile, duplicate-key rejection, number and null rules, schema version, hash domain separation; salt length, custody and opening protocol; Merkle tree construction, leaf order, empty and odd-node rules).
- C7: a separate inference-configuration identity (quantisation, adapters, tokenizer, parameters, routing and fallback), reported beside model identity.
- C17 and C18: a shared action identifier, with tests for duplicate or replayed actions and for a crash just before and just after an external effect.
- C14: request-ID scope (provider, tenant, endpoint) and treatment of retries, cancellations and partial streams.
- A registry of reason codes, with one meaning each.
- Status: Demo will require a published script, pinned revision, recorded environment, expected outputs, negative cases and a mutation-kill record.
